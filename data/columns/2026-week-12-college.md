## Prospect of the week: Jeremiah Smith, WR, Ohio State

We start at the top, because there's no real debate about the top.

Smith is a 6-4, 223-pound receiver who has produced since the week he got to Columbus. As a freshman on a national title team he caught 76 passes for 1,315 yards and 15 touchdowns. In 2025 it was 87 for 1,243 and 12, and a unanimous All-American nod. In September he torched Illinois for 12 catches, 217 yards and four touchdowns, all career highs. He owns the Big Ten's career records for receiving yards and touchdown catches.

Saturday at Nebraska he did it again: 6 catches, 165 yards, 2 touchdowns. The first was a 67-yarder on Ohio State's opening drive from freshman quarterback Luke Fahey. The second was a toe-drag 31-yarder late in the second quarter. That was his 17th touchdown catch of the year, tying Terry Glenn's Ohio State single-season record. It wasn't enough. Ohio State lost 31-28, its fourth loss and its first four-loss regular season since 2011.

That's the part I want you to notice. Ohio State is 7-4, the quarterback is a freshman, and Smith keeps putting up these lines anyway. Everybody in the building knows where the ball is going, and it doesn't matter. That's what a 1.01 looks like.

**For FBP:** whoever picks first takes him. Superflex doesn't change that. A quarterback can fill two spots for us next year, but Smith is the one rookie in this class you'd take over any of them in any format. The real question is what the team at No. 2 does, and I'll get to that.

## The 30,000-foot view

If you only read one section, read this one.

**Quarterback is the story, and it's our story.** FBP goes superflex in 2027, and this is a strong quarterback class arriving at the perfect moment. Oklahoma State's Drew Mestemaker, Miami's Darian Mensah, Notre Dame's CJ Carr, Oregon's Dante Moore and Texas's Arch Manning all sit in the top 14 of the board, with Ole Miss's Trinidad Chambliss right behind. Several could go in the NFL's first round. In a one-QB league you'd wait on all of them. In ours, expect two or three in the top eight, maybe ahead of a back or receiver you'd have loved last year.

**Receiver is the deepest group.** After Smith, Indiana's Charlie Becker leads the next tier, with Rutgers' KJ Duff, Texas's Cam Coleman and Indiana's Nick Marsh close behind. You'll find useful receivers well into the second round.

**Running back is good at the top and shaky in the middle.** Florida's Jadan Baugh is the best of the backs. Missouri's Ahmad Hardy, Ole Miss's Kewan Lacy and Georgia's Nate Frazier give it depth on paper, but Hardy and Lacy have both lost most of this season, so there are real questions heading into spring.

**Tight end is thin.** LSU's Trey'Dez Green and Oregon's Jamari Johnson are about it for early picks. If you need a tight end, don't plan on the draft.

## How the board did this weekend

It was an ugly Saturday for the Big Ten's top teams, and a mixed one for our prospects.

| Prospect | Score | Line |
|---|---|---|
| Smith, WR | L 28-31 | 6 rec, 165 yds, 2 TD |
| Mestemaker, QB | W 30-27 | 30/40, 294 yds, INT |
| Carr, QB | W 24-3 | 19/29, 312 yds, TD, INT |
| Mensah, QB | W 28-20 | 25/37, 241 yds, 2 INT |
| Manning, QB | W 45-3 | 21/25, 287, 2 TD + rush TD |
| Baugh, RB | W 34-27 | 19 car, 76 yds, TD |
| Becker, WR | L 24-27 | 6 rec, 167 yds, TD |
| Coleman, WR | W 45-3 | 1 rec, 16 yds |
| Hardy, RB | W 28-14 | 3 car, 8 yds |

**Mestemaker** was the gutsiest. He was a game-time decision with a strained right shoulder, didn't practice all week, and played the whole game anyway. Oklahoma State trailed 24-7 at the half and won in overtime.

**Carr** threw for 312 and the Irish defense did the rest. Notre Dame is 10-1 and a win at Syracuse should all but lock up a playoff spot.

**Mensah** had the worst stat line of the group: no touchdown passes for the first time this season and two picks, after throwing two all year. Miami's coaches say it was one of his best games anyway, and they have a point. He kept drives alive in a second half where Miami held the ball for 23 of 30 minutes, without his top receiver. He's still at 3,324 yards, 29 touchdowns and four interceptions on the year.

**Manning** went 21 of 25 in three quarters and sat the fourth. That's the version scouts were promised.

**Baugh** didn't have a big day, but he scored the go-ahead touchdown from 10 yards out with 2:18 left. He's at 981 yards and 16 rushing touchdowns, 19 yards from Florida's first back-to-back 1,000-yard seasons.

**Becker** was the best player on the field in a loss. A 74-yard touchdown on Indiana's first snap, 167 yards on seven targets, and his quarterback got benched anyway.

**Coleman** is playing through a shoulder injury and hasn't practiced. He caught the first pass of the game and was targeted once more.

**Hardy** is still being eased back in. He returned November 1 after missing most of the year with a leg injury, and he's had 14 carries since.

**Out for the year:** Ole Miss's Kewan Lacy (shoulder) and Rutgers' KJ Duff (high ankle surgery). Duff is expected to enter the draft. Lacy has a year of eligibility left and hasn't said.

**The race for 1.01 is tight.** Wholey Club has 1,224.9 potential points, Son of Tinactin 1,246.8 and Jesus Christ College 1,271.0. That's 21.9 points between the top two and another 24.2 to third, which is one big week either way. The Fist of the Magi (1,330.9) and Sour Grapes (1,352.9) are further back. Three weeks are left, so all three of those picks are still in play, and the winner gets Smith.

**JCC is the mover,** from fifth to third. Remember, the order runs on potential points, not actual points, so a bad lineup doesn't help you here. A bad roster does. JCC scored a record-low 37.0 last week, and their best possible lineup would only have scored 50.9. The Fist of the Magi's best possible lineup scored 143.6, which is why they slid back to fourth.

**Pick 6 is a playoff question.** Hauk Tua is the best team outside the field and has about a 44 percent chance to get in. If they make it, someone else becomes the sixth pick. The Fist of the Magi (2.7 percent) and Wholey Club (0.4 percent) are hanging on by a thread.

**Some teams are double-dipping.** Wholey Club has 1 and 10. Son of Tinactin has 2 and 8. JCC has 3 and 12. In a superflex year with this many quarterbacks, two first-rounders is a real advantage. If you're picking 2 or 3, you can take the best quarterback on the board and still get a shot at Becker or Baugh with your second pick.

All three of those teams play in the East. The fourth East team, Bullish on da Bears, has no first at all. Their pick belongs to JCC. Bullish leads the division now, but the other three are lining up for next year.

## Familiar faces

Here's the part some of you won't love. All five teams in the top five right now have picked in the top five before. These teams know the neighborhood.

- **Son of Tinactin:** 2nd in 2021 (Javonte Williams), 1st in 2024 (Marvin Harrison Jr.), 3rd in 2025 (TreVeyon Henderson), 3rd in 2026 (Carnell Tate). That's four top-three picks in six drafts. I'm not going to comment on that.
- **Jesus Christ College:** 4th in 2021 (Trey Sermon), 2nd in 2022 (Kenneth Walker III), 1st in 2026 (Jeremiyah Love). In the six drafts on the books, no team has picked 1.01 two years running. They're two spots away.
- **The Fist of the Magi:** 1st in 2021 (Najee Harris), 1st in 2025 (Ashton Jeanty), 5th in 2026 (Jordyn Tyson). The only team with two 1.01s.
- **Sour Grapes:** 3rd in 2023 (Zach Charbonnet), 4th in 2024 (Xavier Worthy), 2nd in 2026 (Makai Lemon).
- **Wholey Club:** 4th in 2022 (Garrett Wilson), 2nd in 2025 (Omarion Hampton). They've never had the first pick. They're holding it right now.
