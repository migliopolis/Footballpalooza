You didn't hear it from me. But the Superflex keeper limit has people talking, and the talk has reached the league office.

Here's the rule as it stands for 2027. Each team can keep two veteran quarterbacks, plus any QB it drafts in that year's rookie draft. A rookie who spent his first season on the taxi squad can be kept into year two, but he takes one of the two spots.

Not everybody loves that last part.

## What people are saying

One executive whose team is already up against the two-QB limit didn't hold back:

> "In practice it means only rookies who are studs in year 1 will get kept and defeats the purpose of making them a draft commodity."

His point: if a second-year QB has to beat out a proven veteran for a keeper spot, nobody's keeping the kid who needs a year to develop. So why spend a pick on him in the first place?

A different source put the case for a fix in one line:

> "It's another incentive to invest in developmental guys."

## What's being floated

A rookie QB who spends his **entire** rookie season on the taxi squad would be exempt from the two-QB limit in year two. A team could keep him as a third quarterback alongside its two veterans. Promote him at any point during his rookie year and the exemption is gone.

There's a limit, too. The version I'm hearing about allows **one exempt QB per team**. Stash two or three rookie QBs on the taxi squad and only one of them gets the pass into the following year. Any others would have to win one of the two regular spots. Nobody gets to turn the taxi squad into a QB warehouse.

Nothing's on paper. Nothing's been decided. But the league office is listening.

## Who it would help right now

Four teams have a rookie QB who has spent the whole season on the taxi squad so far:

- **Son of Tinactin:** Fernando Mendoza, $6 next year
- **MR. FROBOT:** Ty Simpson, $3
- **Hauk Tua:** Drew Allar, $1
- **Bullish on da Bears:** Carson Beck, $1

For three of them it's a cheap lottery ticket they wouldn't have to throw back. For Son of Tinactin it's real. They already have Jordan Love, Jayden Daniels and Bryce Young, and under today's rule Mendoza would be fighting all three for two spots. With the exemption, he's safe, and they only have to move one veteran.

There's a catch, and it's sitting in Las Vegas. Kirk Cousins was traded out of town today. If Mendoza starts getting snaps for the Raiders, Son of Tinactin has a choice: activate him and give up the exemption, or leave his points on the taxi squad and protect his spot for next year. Promote him and they also lose the frozen salary and the sixth-year option. From what I hear, the league office is fine with that trade-off. That's the deal: be patient with the kid, and you get to keep him.

## Rex's take

I'm a draft-picks-are-lottery-tickets guy, and this rule makes the tickets worth buying. Superflex is going to make every quarterback expensive. Letting teams grow one of their own, as long as they actually wait on him, is a fair trade.

## About that seventh playoff spot

Don't forget the other rumor. Word is still that the playoff field could grow to seven teams in 2027: three division winners, three wild cards on record, and a seventh seed off the all-play board, with only the 1 seed getting a bye.

Here's how it would look after week 9:

1. Bullish on da Bears (bye)
2. Fully Cooked vs. 7. Thots & Players
3. MR. FROBOT vs. 6. Hold Timers
4. Mailing It In vs. 5. Hauk Tua

Hold Timers get in on record as the third wild card, and Thots take the all-play spot. The team that would feel it most is Fully Cooked: the 2 seed is a week off today, and under the new setup it's a home game.

If you've got an opinion on either one, take it to the commissioner. I'm just telling you what I hear.
