In eight seasons, 96 teams have chased a Footballpalooza title, and plenty of players have put up monster years along the way. Only two have done all three things a player can do in one season: win MVP, make the All-FBP team and win the championship.

The site calls it the Triple Crown now. You'll find it on the Awards page, and on the Player Pages of the two men who own it.

This year, two more players are in the hunt, and both are chasing history while they're at it.

## The two who did it

**Cooper Kupp, 2021, Mailing It In.** Kupp was making $25 that year and scored 335.4 points in 13 starts, 25.8 a start. It's still the best season a receiver has ever had in this league, and the second-best by anybody. Mailing It In earned a first-round bye, and Kupp picked up where he left off: 21.3 in a 162.9-95.1 semifinal rout of FROBOT, then 21.5 in the title game, a 140.2-111.2 win over Thots & Players. The season wasn't close and neither was the final.

**Tyreek Hill, 2023, MR. FROBOT.** Hill went for 322.7 in 13 starts at $31 and took MVP. FROBOT had a first-round bye, then Hill put up 18.9 in a 123.2-95.1 semifinal win over Thots & Players and 13.6 in the title game, where FROBOT got past Fist of the Magi 135.3-126.6. Not his biggest weeks, but the trophy doesn't ask.

Both seasons sit on the All-Time FBP Team today.

## The one that got away, five times

Here's what makes the crown so hard. Every MVP this league has crowned also made the All-FBP 1st Team. That part comes with the territory. The title is what keeps slipping:

- Christian McCaffrey, 2019, Fist of the Magi: 354.1 points, the best season ever. No title.
- Patrick Mahomes, 2020, Bullish on da Bears: MVP, no title.
- Jalen Hurts, 2022, MR. FROBOT: the best quarterback season ever. No title.
- Lamar Jackson, 2024, Wholey Club: MVP, no title.
- McCaffrey again, 2025, Wholey Club: MVP, no title.

Five MVPs, five teams that came up short in December. One great player can carry you to the playoffs. He can't play all eight spots.

## Gibbs vs. Smith-Njigba

**Jaxon Smith-Njigba, Mailing It In.** 263.2 points in 10 starts, 26.3 a start. He leads the league and he's started every week. He's on bye today, then has three games left. At his pace he finishes around 342. That would pass Kupp's 335.4 for the best receiver season in league history, for the same franchise that rode Kupp to the crown.

**Jahmyr Gibbs, Hauk Tua.** 253.4 points in 9 starts, 28.2 a start, the best rate of anyone in the league. He had his bye in week 6, so he has four games left, starting today against JCC. At his pace he finishes around 366. That would be the best season this league has ever seen, past McCaffrey's 354.1 from 2019.

The MVP goes to the top scorer, and right now it's 9.8 points between them. With Smith-Njigba sitting today, Gibbs could take the lead by tonight. Josh Allen is third at 223.1, close enough to keep an eye on but nobody's betting that way. Both are on the current All-FBP 1st Team, and barring injury, they'll stay there.

So it comes down to the trophy.

## The catch

**Mailing It In** is 6-3-1 with a 91% shot at the playoffs. That's the good news. The schedule is the bad news: FROBOT, Thots & Players and Fully Cooked to close, all of them toss-ups on paper. They're also the defending champions, and Kevin M. has won this thing before with a Triple Crown receiver. He knows exactly what it looks like.

**Hauk Tua** is 5-5, and its playoff odds are 48%, a coin flip. Gibbs has done everything asked of him, and Hauk Tua still sits in the middle of the pack. The next two weeks are JCC, then Bullish on da Bears. I'll leave it there.

That's the twist. The player having the best season in league history might not even make the playoffs. The one with the cleaner path still has to get through three tough weeks, then win every playoff game he plays.

## What's at stake

If Smith-Njigba pulls it off, Mailing It In becomes the first franchise with two Triple Crowns. If Gibbs does it, he does it with the best season this league has ever recorded, on a team that was .500 in late November.

Two players have done it in eight years. By January, one of these two could be the third. Or, like five MVPs before them, they find out the trophy was the hard part.
